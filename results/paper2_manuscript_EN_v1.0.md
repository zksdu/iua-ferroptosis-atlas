# Ferroptosis defense collapse, not activation, defines epithelial susceptibility in intrauterine adhesions: a dual-resolution transcriptomic atlas

**Authors:** Bing Song¹*
**Affiliations:** ¹ The Third Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University, Guangzhou, China

## Abstract

Intrauterine adhesions (IUA, Asherman syndrome) are characterized by endometrial fibrosis and are a leading cause of infertility. Ferroptosis has recently been implicated in IUA endometrial injury, yet whether the diseased endometrium reflects **ferroptosis execution** or a **susceptible state caused by loss of lipid-repair defenses** has not been determined at cell-type resolution. We integrated bulk RNA-seq of severe IUA (GSE224093, 7 vs 7) with a single-cell atlas of the Asherman endometrium (GSE215968; 40,336 AS vs 66,064 mid-secretory control cells across 17 major cell types) and scored four ferroptosis modules (activity, defense, iron load, inflammation) per cell. Bulk ferroptosis scores were **lower** in IUA (p=0.0118) with concordant collapse of the GPX4/DHODH/KEAP1/NCOA4 defense axis and Wnt reprogramming (WNT5A↓, SFRP1↓, DKK1↑; ferroptosis–Wnt ρ=0.859). At single-cell resolution, **epithelium was the only major cell type with concordant dual deterioration** (activity +0.145, defense −0.244, both padj≈0), most severe in the disease-specific AS-epithelium subtype (defense −0.335); **macrophages were the only cell type with significantly elevated iron load** (+0.163, padj=2.5×10⁻⁵; highest absolute level of any type, 0.708); stroma and endothelium adopted a "silent susceptible state"; and ferroptosis–inflammation coupling was **myeloid-anchored** (dendritic cells ρ=0.31, macrophages ρ=0.30 vs epithelium ρ=0.07). HMGB1-axis analysis revealed **compartmentalized redistribution** — collapse in parenchymal compartments (epithelium −0.155) concurrent with myeloid activation (dendritic cells +0.291; macrophages +0.114) — explaining the bulk-level decrease. A direction-level concordance check in an independent within-patient IUA cohort (GSE160633, 8 patients pooled 1v1) reproduced defense collapse (11/12 defense genes lower; SLC7A11 0.09×) and absence of execution markers (ACSL4 0.28×, PTGS2 0.25×). Paired pre/post-therapy biopsies from the same phase 1/2 stem-cell trial that generated the atlas (GSE311899, n=9) provided clinical context but did not resolve epithelial defense-axis recovery at mRNA level, which we report as an honest limitation; regenerating stroma showed a coordinated up-shift of both ferroptosis modules led by SLC7A11, a hypothesis-generating observation. Sample-level re-analysis (7/7 direction-consistent; epithelial defense collapse p=0.0067, Hedges g=−1.21) confirmed robustness, as did leave-one-out gene-set perturbation (52/52 runs direction-consistent), cross-scoring with an external KEGG ferroptosis gene set, and composition-adjusted re-testing of the bulk layer (ferroptosis decrease persisting at p=0.0015 after epithelial-fraction adjustment). Our transcriptomic analyses therefore support the interpretation that the dominant event in IUA epithelium is **loss of ferroptosis defenses marking a putatively reversible susceptible state**, embedded in a fibrotic niche where myeloid cells accumulate iron — a cross-disease transcriptional signature shared with preeclamptic placentas. Therapeutically, these observations argue for **restoring epithelial defense** rather than inducing ferroptotic death — a causal model that awaits functional validation in experimental systems.

**Keywords:** intrauterine adhesions; Asherman syndrome; ferroptosis; GPX4; macrophages; endometrium; single-cell RNA-seq

## 1 Introduction

Intrauterine adhesions (IUA), first described by Asherman in 1948 [1], are characterized by the formation of fibrous bands within the endometrial cavity, menstrual disturbance, and infertility [2,3]. The current mainstay of treatment, hysteroscopic adhesiolysis, restores cavity patency [4] but is followed by high rates of re-adhesion and modest reproductive outcomes, because the underlying endometrial repair program remains defective [3,5]. The molecular events that lock the injured endometrium into a fibrotic, regeneration-incompetent state are still incompletely understood.

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of regulated necrotic cell death driven by unchecked phospholipid peroxidation and defined by the failure of lipid-repair systems, with glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) at its center [6,7,8,9]. Subsequent work uncovered parallel defense layers, including the FSP1–coenzyme Q10 pathway [10,11] and the dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) arm [12], establishing "ferroptosis defense capacity" as a tunable property of every cell type [13,14]. Because execution of ferroptosis and loss of ferroptosis defenses can dissociate, the transcriptomic footprint of a tissue may report either true death execution or a primed, vulnerable state — two readings with opposite therapeutic implications.

Ferroptosis entered the IUA field through the study of Zhu et al., who reported elevated 4-hydroxynonenal with reduced GPX4 in injured epithelium (4v4 bulk RNA-seq plus immunohistochemistry), and who showed in mice that the ferroptosis inhibitor ferrostatin-1 ameliorates endometrial fibrosis [15]. Their interpretation placed ferroptosis burden upstream of epithelial–mesenchymal transition and fibrosis, in parallel with TGFβ/autophagy-driven stromal–myofibroblast transition as an independent fibrotic channel in IUA [16]. What remains unsettled, however, is whether the human transcriptomic evidence supports **execution** of ferroptosis in the diseased endometrium, or rather a **susceptible state produced by collapse of lipid-repair defenses** — and, critically, no study has resolved these events at cell-type resolution in the human endometrium.

Here we address this question with a dual-resolution design. We re-analyzed the bulk RNA-seq cohort submitted by Zhu et al. (GSE224093, severe IUA 7 vs 7) [15] and a 106,400-cell single-cell atlas of the Asherman endometrium (GSE215968) [17], scored four ferroptosis-related modules (pro-ferroptotic activity, lipid-repair defense, iron handling, inflammation/HMGB1 axis) in every cell type, and verified the direction of change in an independent within-patient cohort (GSE160633, thin adhesive vs adjacent normal endometrium, 8 patients; strictly direction-level evidence owing to the 1v1 pooled design), and finally interrogated paired pre/post-therapy biopsies (GSE311899) from the same phase 1/2 trial of autologous CD133+ bone-marrow-derived stem cell (BMDSC) therapy that generated the single-cell atlas [17,18,19].

Our analysis converges on a reinterpretation: the dominant transcriptomic event in IUA epithelium is not ferroptosis execution but **collapse of ferroptosis defenses defining a susceptible epithelial state**, embedded in a fibrotic niche in which myeloid cells constitute the iron sink — a myeloid-anchored iron niche that we observe in parallel analyses of preeclamptic placenta [20,21] and propose here as a testable model of fibrotic microenvironments for further experimental validation.

## 2 Materials and methods

### 2.1 Data sources and ethics

All datasets were obtained from public Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) archives of de-identified human tissue: GSE224093 (endometrial bulk RNA-seq, severe IUA n=7 vs controls n=7, submitted by Zhu et al. [15]); GSE215968 (single-cell atlas of the Asherman endometrium and mid-secretory controls, generated by Santamaria et al. [17]); GSE160633 (thin adhesive vs adjacent normal endometrium, 8 patients, 1v1 pooled RNA-seq); and GSE311899 (paired pre/post-therapy biopsies from a phase 1/2 trial of autologous CD133+ BMDSC therapy for moderate-to-severe Asherman syndrome, EudraCT 2016-003975-23, archived 2025/12) [18]. The overall design integrating these four cohorts at two resolutions is summarized in Figure 1. The original studies were conducted with their respective ethics approvals and informed consent; the present work is a secondary analysis of public data and required no additional ethical approval.

### 2.2 Bulk RNA-seq processing (GSE224093)

Counts-independent expression matrices were processed as log2(FPKM+1). Differentially expressed genes were tested with Welch's t-test and Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) correction. Pathway activity was quantified with a single-sample gene-set scoring scheme in the spirit of singscore [22] and GSVA [23] using curated gene sets: a 64-gene ferroptosis panel from FerrDb V2 [24], a 20-gene HMGB1 axis panel, a 16-gene Notch panel, and a 39-gene Wnt panel (Supplementary Table S1). Sample-wise associations between pathway scores were assessed by Spearman correlation (n=14).

### 2.3 Single-cell processing (GSE215968)

We used the published atlas of Santamaria et al. [17]: 106,400 cells × 33,538 genes, with 17 major cell types annotated by the original authors, consistent with reference single-cell maps of the cycling endometrium [25]. Four module gene sets were applied (pro-ferroptotic activity, 13 genes; ferroptosis defense, 12; iron handling, 7; inflammation/HMGB1 axis, 10; full gene list in Supplementary Table S1). Genes were z-scored per cell and module scores computed as the mean of member-gene z-scores; between-group differences within each cell type were tested with the Mann–Whitney U test and BH correction. Robustness at sample level was assessed by treating each sample × cell-type median score as one observation (Welch's t-test, Mann–Whitney U, and Hedges' g). The 20-gene HMGB1 axis (gene list in Supplementary Table S5) was processed identically; full per-cell-type statistics are provided in Supplementary Table S3.

### 2.4 Direction-level validation (GSE160633)

For each of 8 IUA patients, thin adhesive endometrium and adjacent normal endometrium were each pooled in equal quantity (1v1 design). We computed FPKM ratios (adhesive/normal) per gene and scored the direction of change across the three ferroptosis modules. Given the 1v1 pooling, no significance testing was performed and results are reported strictly as direction-level concordance.

### 2.5 Paired pre/post-therapy cohort (GSE311899)

Nine patients with moderate-to-severe Asherman syndrome underwent intraoperative endometrial biopsy before and after autologous CD133+ BMDSC therapy, microdissected into epithelial-enriched and stromal compartments (35 profiles; 236,867 cells in total; 8 complete epithelial pairs and 9 stromal pairs after quality filtering). Each sample was aggregated into a pseudobulk profile (sum of counts → log1p counts-per-million) and the four modules scored as the mean log1p CPM of member genes. Paired Pre-versus-Post differences were tested with the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, with the paired t-test as sensitivity analysis; per-gene direction was summarized as mean paired delta and the fraction of patients with an increase. The full per-sample pseudobulk matrix is provided in Supplementary Table S4.

### 2.6 Statistics and reproducibility

Analyses were performed in Python 3.13 (scipy, statsmodels, matplotlib) and R 4.6.1. All scripts and intermediate products are archived at GitHub (https://github.com/zksdu/iua-ferroptosis-atlas) and Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23029283). Multiple testing was controlled by BH correction within each analysis layer; exact n and tests are stated for every comparison. A cohort originating from the same clinical trial as the single-cell atlas (GSE311899) is analyzed and reported explicitly as therapeutic-response evidence rather than independent replication.

### 2.7 Robustness and confounding analyses

Because the four module gene sets are curated, three robustness layers were added. (i) **Leave-one-out (LOO) sensitivity**: for each module, member genes were removed one at a time, per-cell module scores recomputed, and the key cell-type-level conclusions re-tested (Mann–Whitney U), reporting the fraction of perturbations in which direction and significance (p<0.05) were preserved. (ii) **External gene-set cross-scoring**: cells were re-scored with the KEGG "Ferroptosis" gene set (hsa04216, 16 genes present in the atlas) as an independent, non-curated definition, and directions were compared with the curated modules. (iii) **Composition control for the bulk layer**: cell-compartments were estimated in GSE224093 with an MCP-counter-style single-sample scheme (mean z-score of canonical marker genes per compartment: epithelial EPCAM/KRT, stromal DCN/LUM/COL, endothelial PECAM1/VWF, myeloid LYZ/CD68/CD14, lymphoid CD3/MS4A1/NKG7), and pathway-score group effects were re-estimated by linear regression (score ~ IUA + compartment scores, n=14). In addition, the Wnt–ferroptosis coupling was localized by computing per-cell Spearman correlation within each cell type, and the myeloid compartment was sub-typed with M1 (IL1B/TNF/CXCL9/10/CD80/CCR7/STAT1/IRF5) and M2 (MRC1/CD163/MSR1/ARG1/TGFB1/MERTK/CCL18/TREM2) polarization signatures correlated against iron-load, HMGB1-axis and inflammation scores. For the cross-disease parallel, per-cell-type module statistics from the preeclampsia maternal–fetal-interface atlas re-processed in our parallel work with the identical four modules and scoring scheme were re-used (Mann–Whitney U, BH correction; Supplementary Figure S7, Supplementary Table S6). All outputs are provided in Supplementary Table S6 and Supplementary Figures S3–S7. A formal genome-wide GSEA of the therapy cohort was not feasible because the deposited pseudobulk resources are limited to the curated panels (37 genes), which is insufficient for gene-set ranking; the module-level paired analyses in Section 3.7 carry this reading.

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Bulk ferroptosis scores are reduced in severe IUA, with defense-gene collapse and Wnt reprogramming

Contrary to the expectation that ferroptosis execution would elevate pathway activity, the 64-gene ferroptosis score was significantly **lower** in severe IUA than in controls (0.535 vs 0.564, p=0.0118, padj=0.0235). The decrease was accompanied by concordant down-regulation of the lipid-repair defense axis: DHODH (p=0.009), KEAP1 (p=0.014) and NCOA4 (p=0.022) were significantly lower, with GPX4 (p=0.10) and TFRC (p=0.08) lower in direction; iron-storage genes FTH1/FTL were modestly higher (ns). Wnt circuitry was reprogrammed in parallel: WNT5A (p=0.004) and SFRP1 (p=0.035) decreased, the Wnt score decreased (p=0.0036), and the canonical Wnt antagonist DKK1 increased (p=0.099); notably, the ferroptosis and Wnt scores were strongly coupled across samples (ρ=0.859, p<0.01). The fibrosis marker ACTA2 was directionally elevated (log2FC=+1.22, p=0.094). At gene level, only 4 of 33,944 tested genes survived genome-wide correction (padj<0.05 and |log2FC|>0.5; all 7 genes with padj<0.05 are listed in Supplementary Table S2) — an expected consequence of the 7v7 design — so gene-level inference relies on nominal p-values with direction consistency, while the pathway level carries the primary statistical signal (Figure 2).

### 3.2 Single-cell resolution identifies epithelium as the only dual-hit major cell type

In the 106,400-cell atlas (published by Santamaria et al. [17]; re-analyzed here for ferroptosis/inflammation modules, not an independent replication), epithelium was the **only** major cell type with concordant dual deterioration: pro-ferroptotic activity increased (+0.145, padj≈0) while ferroptosis defense collapsed (−0.244, padj≈0). The disease-specific AS-epithelium injury subtype showed the deepest defense collapse of any epithelial population (−0.335, padj=1.2×10⁻³⁴), followed by ciliated cells (−0.211, padj=1.8×10⁻⁶). At population scale this reproduces the epithelial focus of Zhu et al. [15] while reinterpreting it as a **susceptible rather than an executing state** (Figure 3).

### 3.3 Macrophages are the iron sink of the fibrotic endometrium

Macrophages were the only cell type with significantly elevated iron-load score (+0.163, padj=2.5×10⁻⁵) and simultaneously held the highest absolute iron-load level of any cell type (0.708); their pro-ferroptotic activity was also elevated (+0.122, p=0.0035). This "iron sink" phenotype mirrors the ferroptosis-loaded Hofbauer cells we observe in preeclamptic placenta (padj=1.9×10⁻¹⁷), and aligns with the established role of iron-handling macrophages in scavenging ferroptotic debris [21] and the central role of macrophages in tissue repair and fibrosis [26] (Figure 4).

### 3.4 Stroma and endothelium adopt a silent susceptible state

Stromal cells collapsed their defense module (−0.137, padj≈0) without inducing activity (−0.048); endothelium showed the second-deepest defense collapse (−0.250, padj≈0), and the disease-specific AS uterine smooth-muscle population collapsed likewise (−0.208). Defense collapse without execution signals defines a **silent susceptible state**, consistent with the observation of Zhu et al. that erastin-treated epithelial cells promote fibrosis of human endometrial stromal cells only indirectly, via paracellular signaling [15] (Figure 4).

### 3.5 Ferroptosis–inflammation coupling is myeloid-anchored

Across the 17 cell types, the correlation between pro-ferroptotic activity and the inflammation module was anchored in myeloid populations: dendritic cells ρ=0.31 and macrophages ρ=0.30, versus endothelium 0.18, stroma 0.14, and epithelium 0.07. The same myeloid anchoring is seen in our parallel preeclampsia analyses (Hofbauer ρ=0.39; six-of-six spatial transcriptomic hot spots macrophage-coupled), supporting a cross-disease, **myeloid-anchored ferroptosis niche** (Figure 5). Erythrocytes showed the numerically highest rho (0.50, n=174); because these are anucleate cells whose profile reflects residual erythroid transcripts rather than a transcriptional regulatory program, we regard their coupling as non-informative and restrict the anchoring claim to nucleated lineages, among which myeloid cells rank highest (Figure 5).

### 3.6 The HMGB1 axis shows compartmentalized redistribution, not global elevation

At the bulk level the 20-gene HMGB1 axis trended **down** in IUA (p=0.093), with HMGB1 itself significantly lower (log2FC=−0.37, p=0.0019) — an apparent negative that must be confronted. Single-cell re-analysis resolved the paradox as **compartmentalized redistribution**: the axis collapsed in eight parenchymal populations (ciliated −0.203, epithelium −0.155, AS-uAS-uSMC −0.155, AS-epithelium −0.120, endothelium −0.094, stroma −0.075, perivascular −0.056, B cells −0.053; all padj<0.05) while rising in seven immune populations (dendritic cells +0.291, padj=4.1×10⁻⁵; macrophages +0.114, p=2.6×10⁻³; cytotoxic NK +0.155; CD8 +0.083; MAIT +0.065; DN T +0.052; NK +0.042). The bulk decrease is thus an artifact of cellular composition — parenchymal mass dilutes a myeloid-restricted activation signal — and the HMGB1 axis joins the myeloid-anchored niche as supporting evidence rather than contradicting it [27,28,29,30] (Figure 6).

### 3.7 Paired pre/post-therapy biopsies do not resolve epithelial defense-axis recovery (honest negative with a stromal signal)

To probe reversibility, we analyzed an additional paired biopsy cohort from the same phase 1/2 trial of autologous CD133+ BMDSC therapy (EudraCT 2016-003975-23) that generated the single-cell atlas above [17,18]; this cohort is therefore not independent, contains no healthy controls, and all results should be read as therapeutic-response evidence rather than replication. Nine patients yielded 35 microdissected profiles (236,867 cells; 8 complete epithelial and 9 stromal Pre/Post pairs).

In the **epithelial compartment** (n=8 pairs) we found no recovery signal: the defense module was unchanged after therapy (9.184 → 9.168; Δ=−0.016, paired Wilcoxon p=0.46), pro-ferroptotic activity was flat (+0.021, p=0.64), and iron handling (−0.161, p=0.11) and inflammation (−0.278, p=0.38) were not significant. Gene-level directions were inconsistent: 5 of 12 epithelial defense genes rose (SLC7A11 in 7/8 patients, Δ+0.556; NQO1 in 7/8, Δ+0.325), but HMOX1 fell in 8/8 patients (Δ−0.941) and the execution marker ACSL4 unexpectedly rose in 6/8 (Δ+0.447) — not a recovery signature.

In the **stromal compartment** (n=9 pairs), both modules rose significantly and together: pro-ferroptotic activity +0.487 (p=0.027; paired t p=0.027) and defense +0.279 (p=0.039; t p=0.056), with 10 of 12 stromal defense genes increased, led by SLC7A11 (Δ+1.60, 8/9 patients). Because the pro-ferroptotic rise exceeded the defense rise while iron handling and inflammation remained flat, this pattern is more consistent with a global transcriptional reactivation of regenerating stroma than with a specific repair of the ferroptosis axis; we report it as hypothesis-generating only. Post-therapy samples also yielded more cells per sample (epithelium 4,603 → 7,602; stroma 5,557 → 9,068); module scores are within-sample means and cell number should not bias their direction, but sub-compartmental composition shifts cannot be excluded in pseudobulk analysis.

We therefore report an **honest negative**: the prediction that epithelial defense recovers with endometrial regeneration is not supported at mRNA level in this cohort. The reversibility corollary of our framework rests on the murine ferrostatin-1 evidence of Zhu et al. [15] and on the trial-reported clinical regeneration itself [17,19], not on transcriptomic confirmation here. The consistent SLC7A11 rise in stroma (and its direction in 7/8 epithelial pairs) merits follow-up at single-cell resolution (Figure 7A; per-gene heatmap in Figure S2).

### 3.8 Direction-level concordance in an independent within-patient cohort (GSE160633)

In the 8-patient 1v1 pooled cohort, adhesive endometrium reproduced the defense-collapsed, non-executing signature: 11 of 12 defense genes were lower (fraction 0.917; SLC7A11 0.090×, DHODH 0.714×, GPX4 0.766×, FTH1 0.701×, HMOX1 0.523×, TFRC 0.327×), and — critically — the execution markers were **not** elevated (ACSL4 0.284×, PTGS2 0.246×; 8 of 10 pro-ferroptotic genes lower). Iron-handling genes were lower in 5 of 7 cases. Given the pooled design these are strictly direction-level observations, but they independently reproduce the susceptible-state signature in within-patient tissue (Figure 7B).

### 3.9 Sample-level robustness

All cell-type findings survived re-analysis at sample level: direction consistency in 7/7 control-vs-IUA comparisons; epithelial defense collapse p=0.0067 (Mann–Whitney p=0.0071; Hedges g=−1.21); endothelial defense collapse p=0.0043 (g=−1.40); AS-epithelium g=−3.09 (Welch p=0.013; Mann–Whitney p=0.057, limited by n=3 vs 4). Comparisons with restricted sample sizes and non-significant statistics are reported as such (epithelial activity g=0.21, p=0.60; macrophage iron load g=0.56, p=0.36; n=6 vs 3). The full forest of effect sizes is shown in Figure S1.

### 3.10 Robustness, composition control, and myeloid substructure

**Leave-one-out sensitivity.** Across 52 single-gene perturbations of the four module panels, every key cell-type conclusion retained both direction and magnitude: epithelial defense collapse 12/12 runs (full-panel Δ=−0.244, all p<1e−4), epithelial activity gain 13/13 (Δ=+0.145), macrophage iron-load gain 7/7 (Δ=+0.163, padj=1.2×10⁻⁵), and macrophage HMGB1-axis gain 20/20 direction-consistent (18/20 with p<0.05; removing TLR2 or NLRP3 weakened significance to p=0.054/0.104 without changing direction). No single gene drives any headline claim (Supplementary Figure S3, Supplementary Table S6).

**External gene-set cross-scoring.** The independent KEGG Ferroptosis set (16 genes in-data) reproduced the direction pattern of the curated panels: lower in epithelium (Δ=−0.034, padj=9.3×10⁻¹⁹) and stroma (Δ=−0.074, padj≈0), and nominally higher in macrophages (Δ=+0.029, p=0.049) — the "reduced ferroptosis transcription in parenchyma, elevated in myeloid" architecture does not depend on our gene curation.

**Wnt–ferroptosis coupling is epithelial/endothelial, not myeloid.** Per-cell-type correlation located the Wnt–ferroptosis coupling primarily in endothelium (ρ=0.186) and epithelium (ρ=0.176; ciliated 0.166; all padj<1×10⁻¹⁵), with weak coupling in stroma (0.044) and macrophages (0.065) — consistent with a cell-autonomous Wnt-defense interplay in the susceptible epithelial compartment rather than a myeloid-mediated phenomenon (Supplementary Figure S5).

**Myeloid substructure.** Within macrophages, the HMGB1-axis and inflammation scores correlated strongly with the M1 polarization signature (ρ=0.643 and 0.806) and only weakly with M2 (ρ=0.186/0.065); iron-load correlated with M1 (ρ=0.269). AS macrophages were depleted of the M2 signature (median Δ=−0.571, padj=3.4×10⁻⁵) while dendritic cells shifted toward M2-like polarization (Δ=+0.962, padj=2.7×10⁻⁶) — the myeloid activation in IUA is M1-polarization-associated rather than a general phagocyte program (Supplementary Figure S6).

**Bulk composition control.** Marker-based compartment scores showed the IUA bulk microenvironment is not explained by epithelial loss (epithelial score unchanged, p=1.0) but by significant myeloid enrichment (p=0.0070; stroma and endothelium nominally higher, p=0.053/0.073). The ferroptosis-score decrease remained significant after adjustment for epithelial fraction (group β=−0.027, p=0.0015) and for full compartment composition (p=0.0042), and the Wnt decrease likewise persisted (p=0.0040). In contrast, the bulk HMGB1-axis decrease vanished after composition adjustment (p=0.09 unadjusted → p=0.77), directly supporting the compositional-dilution interpretation of Section 3.6 (Supplementary Figure S4).

**Cross-disease parallel (IUA vs preeclampsia), same modules.** Re-using per-cell-type statistics from the preeclampsia placenta atlas scored with the identical four modules and scheme localized the shared and divergent components of the proposed myeloid-anchored iron niche (Supplementary Figure S7, Supplementary Table S6). **Shared**: macrophage-lineage iron accumulation — the single most concordant cross-disease signal (IUA macrophages Δ=+0.163, padj≈0; Hofbauer cells Δ=+0.171, padj=1.9×10⁻¹⁷, near-identical magnitude) — and parenchymal defense collapse (IUA epithelium −0.244; trophoblast −0.109; both padj≈0). **Divergent**: the direction of parenchymal pro-ferroptotic activity (IUA epithelium +0.145 vs trophoblast −0.170) and macrophage-level inflammation deltas (IUA ns, +0.078; Hofbauer −0.211, whose myeloid anchoring in placenta lies in the ferroptosis–inflammation coupling, ρ=0.39, rather than in the group delta). The cross-disease claim is therefore restricted to the iron-sink-plus-defense-collapse architecture, not to a uniform ferroptosis-execution program (Figure S7).

## 4 Discussion

### 4.1 A susceptible state, not ferroptosis execution

The central reinterpretation proposed here is that the dominant transcriptomic event in IUA endometrium is not execution of ferroptosis but a **susceptible state marked by collapse of ferroptosis defenses**. This reading does not contradict Zhu et al. [15]; it extends it. Their immunohistochemistry showed raised 4-HNE with reduced GPX4 in injured epithelium — which in our framework is direct protein-level evidence of defense collapse, with raised 4-HNE as the early consequence of lipid peroxidation escaping control, short of irreversible execution. A further isomorphic observation strengthens this reading: erastin, the tool used by Zhu et al. to induce epithelial ferroptosis experimentally, is itself a system x<sub>c</sub><sup>−</sup> inhibitor — an agent that manufactures defense collapse. In their experiments stromal cells (HESC) did not respond to erastin directly but were pushed toward fibrosis by conditioned medium from treated epithelium, exactly the structure expected if defense collapse arises in epithelium and fibrosis is a paracrine sequel. Three independent strata of evidence — population bulk (lower ferroptosis score, p=0.0118, with concordant DHODH/KEAP1/NCOA4 down-regulation), single-cell resolution (epithelial dual hit with stromal/endothelial silence), and the independent direction-level cohort (defense 11/12 lower with execution markers equally lower) — converge on the same conclusion: describing IUA as "ferroptosis executed" lacks transcriptomic support, whereas the defense-collapsed susceptible state explains all observations simultaneously. This reading is also consistent with the context-dependent, double-edged roles of ferroptosis reported across female reproductive diseases [31,32].

### 4.2 The epithelial dual hit and its therapeutic corollary

Epithelium was the only major cell type combining raised pro-ferroptotic activity with lost defense capacity, and the disease-specific AS-epithelium subtype collapsed most deeply (−0.335). This dual hit makes epithelium the cell type most likely to cross the execution threshold upon any further oxidative challenge, while the intact tissue has not yet broadly executed. The therapeutic implication differs sharply between the two readings: if ferroptosis were already executed, inhibitors would arrive too late; a defense-collapsed but viable state instead argues for **restoring epithelial defenses** (GPX4/System x<sub>c</sub><sup>−</sup> axis agonism; donor-derived exosomal delivery of defensive cargo). Our paired post-therapy biopsies did not confirm epithelial defense recovery at mRNA level (Section 3.7, honest negative), so reversibility currently rests on the murine ferrostatin-1 results of Zhu et al. [15] and the trial-reported clinical regeneration [17,19]; the consistent stromal — and directional epithelial — rise of SLC7A11 nominates the system x<sub>c</sub><sup>−</sup> axis as the earliest responsive defense node during regeneration and a target for single-cell follow-up. The combination proposed by Zhu et al. (blocking execution with ferrostatin-1) and our framework (repairing defenses upstream) are complementary arms of a single "prevent execution + restore defense" strategy.

### 4.3 The myeloid iron niche: a cross-disease signature

Macrophages were the only cell type with significantly raised iron load and the highest absolute level of any type — the iron sink. Ferroptosis–inflammation coupling was anchored in myeloid cells (dendritic cells 0.31; macrophages 0.30; epithelium 0.07), mirroring our parallel preeclampsia analyses (Hofbauer cells padj=1.9×10⁻¹⁷ for iron load; ρ=0.39 for coupling; six of six spatial hot spots macrophage-anchored). That the same architecture — iron-accumulating myeloid cells coupled to inflammatory output within a fibrotic, ischemic microenvironment — recurs in two distinct pregnancy-related diseases suggests a **shared myeloid-anchored iron niche** of fibrotic tissue rather than disease-specific phenomena [20,21]. Re-scoring the preeclampsia atlas with the identical modules quantified this: macrophage-lineage iron accumulation is the most concordant cross-disease signal (IUA +0.163 vs Hofbauer +0.171, near-identical magnitude), together with parenchymal defense collapse, while the parenchymal activity direction diverges — the shared claim is therefore the iron-sink-plus-defense-collapse architecture, not a uniform ferroptosis program (Supplementary Figure S7). Myeloid subtyping sharpened this picture: the HMGB1-axis activation of IUA macrophages tracks the M1 polarization signature (ρ=0.643) rather than a general phagocyte program, with M2 signature depletion in AS macrophages and an opposite M2-like shift in dendritic cells — a polarized, not merely expanded, myeloid compartment. The HMGB1 redistribution observed here (parenchymal collapse, myeloid activation) adds a mechanistic channel: iron-loading M1-polarized macrophages both clear ferroptotic debris, consistent with their sink role, and emit HMGB1/IL1B-centered paracrine signals, consistent with the paracrine dependence of stromal fibrosis in the erastin system [15,27,28,29,30].

### 4.4 Wnt–ferroptosis crosstalk and the regeneration narrative

The strong coupling between ferroptosis and Wnt scores (ρ=0.859) connects our findings to the endometrial regeneration literature. Localizing this coupling within the single-cell atlas showed it is anchored in endothelium (ρ=0.186) and epithelium (ρ=0.176) rather than in myeloid cells (ρ=0.065) — a correlative co-expression pattern, not a causal claim, that places the Wnt–ferroptosis interplay inside the susceptible epithelial compartment itself. The Wnt reprogramming of IUA (WNT5A/SFRP1 down, DKK1 up) is a recognized fibrosis-associated shift, and Wnt signaling intersects iron and glutathione metabolism in ways that can amplify epithelial susceptibility. This coupling suggests that restoring Wnt polarity and restoring ferroptosis defenses may need to proceed together, providing a ferroptosis-dimension mechanistic supplement to the Wnt–ferroptosis crosstalk described in cancer biology [33] and to stem-cell and exosome-based therapies whose core action is precisely Wnt-centered niche reconstruction, grounded in endometrial stem/progenitor cell biology [5,34,35,36,37].

### 4.5 Alternative hypotheses considered

Because all evidence here is observational, we explicitly weighed alternative readings of the same transcriptomic phenomena.

*Hypothesis A — the defense collapse reflects selection, not susceptibility: cells that had already executed ferroptosis died and sloughed, so the remaining epithelium simply transcripts lower defense genes.* Our data cannot fully exclude survivor selection. Three observations argue against widespread prior execution, however: (i) the pro-ferroptotic module rose concordantly in the same epithelial populations, a pattern expected of a primed state rather than of a debris field; (ii) in the independent within-patient cohort the execution markers ACSL4 and PTGS2 were not elevated (0.28× and 0.25×, direction-level); and (iii) no broad execution signature emerged in any epithelial subtype. Distinguishing dead-and-shed epithelium from defense-collapsed viable epithelium will require histology-oriented experiments (e.g., 4-HNE/GPX4 co-immunofluorescence with epithelial lineage markers) and is beyond transcriptomic resolution.

*Hypothesis B — myeloid iron loading is a consequence, not a contributor, of fibrosis.* We observe only correlation: transcriptomics cannot order myeloid iron accumulation relative to fibrosis onset. The cross-disease recurrence (IUA endometrium and preeclamptic placenta) and the myeloid anchoring of ferroptosis–inflammation coupling make a contributory role plausible, but causality requires functional models (e.g., macrophage-specific iron-handling perturbation).

*Hypothesis C — module score shifts reflect cell-composition changes rather than per-cell transcriptional shifts.* This alternative is directly addressed: all single-cell comparisons are within-cell-type (Mann–Whitney on per-cell scores, BH-corrected), the sample-level re-analysis treats each sample × cell-type median as one observation, and the bulk layer is examined in the same direction as the within-cell-type signals rather than interpreted alone.

*Hypothesis D — the leave-one-cycle-stage confound drives the epithelial signal.* Controls are mid-secretory; if ferroptosis-defense transcription varies across the cycle, part of the epithelial contrast could be stage-related. The original atlas controls for cycle phase at the annotation level, but residual stage effects cannot be excluded at module level; the concordant direction in the bulk cohort (unstratified for cycle) and in the within-patient cohort argues that cycle stage alone is unlikely to explain the signature.

### 4.6 Interpretation boundaries: observation, inference, hypothesis

We keep three layers of claims explicitly separate. **Transcriptomic observations** (established here): ferroptosis defense modules are lower in IUA epithelium, stroma and endothelium at single-cell resolution; macrophages alone show elevated iron-load scores; ferroptosis–inflammation coupling is myeloid-anchored; the HMGB1 axis is redistributed, not globally elevated; paired post-therapy epithelium shows no mRNA-level defense recovery. **Plausible interpretations** (supported by these observations plus prior literature): IUA epithelium resides in a defense-collapsed, putatively susceptible state; myeloid cells form an iron sink coupled to inflammatory output within the fibrotic niche. **Hypotheses for future testing** (not established by this study): that defense restoration prevents fibrosis progression or adhesion re-formation; that the myeloid-anchored iron niche causally sustains fibrosis; that epithelial susceptibility is reversible under regenerative therapy. Sentences in Sections 4.1–4.4 are worded against this three-layer scheme.

### 4.7 Limitations

(1) The 7v7 bulk cohort has limited power; gene-level conclusions rely on nominal p-values with direction consistency, and pathway-level significance requires larger cohorts for confirmation. (2) All evidence is mRNA-level; transcript abundance is not equivalent to protein abundance, enzyme activity, or the actual degree of lipid peroxidation, so statements here are restricted to mRNA terms (e.g., "GPX4 mRNA expression is lower", never "GPX4 activity is reduced"); protein-level confirmation rests on the IHC of Zhu et al. and on future assays (iron staining, 4-HNE/GPX4 immunofluorescence, BODIPY-C11). (3) GSE160633 is a 1v1 pooled design providing direction-level evidence only, with no statistical power. (4) GSE311899 derives from the same team's clinical trial, lacks healthy controls, and supports only therapeutic-response reading — it cannot substitute for independent disease–control replication; its honest negative does not exclude recovery at the protein or post-transcriptional level, which mRNA profiling cannot observe. (5) The GSE215968 controls are mid-secretory (window of implantation) endometrium; cycle confounding was managed by the original authors but residual confounding cannot be excluded. (6) Module scores are relative transcriptional measures, not direct biological measurements of ferroptosis; their robustness was therefore assessed by leave-one-out gene-set sensitivity analysis and by cross-scoring with an external KEGG ferroptosis gene set (Supplementary Figures S3–S4, Supplementary Table S6), but residual gene-set bias cannot be excluded. (7) Cross-dataset comparisons are subject to compositional effects, which we mitigated by within-cell-type testing and sample-level robustness analysis. (8) The cross-disease comparison with preeclamptic placenta is a parallel transcriptomic observation only; it does not establish the myeloid-anchored iron niche as a universal driver of fibrosis.
